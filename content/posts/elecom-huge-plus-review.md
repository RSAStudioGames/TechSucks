I was never much one for trackball mice, they always felt too slow and clunky for my tastes, but that was before I received the Elecom Huge Plus, and I am very happy with my purchase.

Let me start with the things I don’t like about it. For one, you need to occasionally lubricate the rollers in the mouse to keep it moving smoothly. It occasionally ‘sticks’ a bit for very short horizontal movements due to the triangular positioning of the three rollers when they aren’t regularly maintained. And unlike most other mice I’ve used, trackballs require (minimal and very simple) maintenance to keep them going smoothly. But besides those few issues, it is a phenomenal mouse.

First impressions: It’s absolutely physically HUGE (plus), heavy, feels sturdy, and makes cursor movement a breeze. I’ve used thumb trackballs in the past; however, the Huge Plus is operated with the index or middle finger. The trackball operation reminds me of a cross between the simplicity of a touchpad and trackpoint merged into one.
Also, despite not having official support for Linux, it worked right out of the box in all three operation modes on my computer. However, their mouse assistant software is not available on Linux, but there do seem to be some workarounds which I did not try myself.

The Huge Plus is responsive, has comfortable ergonomics, and feels very snappy. The buttons on the mouse feel good to press; clicky while still managing to be almost entirely silent which is quite surprising as most ‘quiet’ mice buttons feel very mushy and sometimes downright gross, like a limp handshake. It has plenty of extra buttons that you can assign custom functions to via the [Elecom Mouse Assistant](https://www.elecom.co.jp/global/download-list/utility/mouse_assistant/win/) software. There are three DPI modes for the cursor movement speeds; 500, 1,000, and 1,500 DPI.
There are three operation modes: Bluetooth, USB dongle, or wired. The cable it comes with is not very flexible (relative to traditional mice), but since you aren’t moving the mouse around, it's not restrictive to cursor movement or overall usage. Additionally, the two buttons on the bottom allow you to toggle wireless modes. 'L' for low and 'H' for high. These change the wireless range of the mouse while in Bluetooth or wireless dongle mode. On low mode, the wireless range will be shorter, which isn’t an issue if you have the device close to the dongle/computer, and will have a longer battery span of around 5 months. On high mode, you will have higher wireless range, which would penetrate surfaces and walls better if you’re noticing lag or stuttering, and the battery life would be shorter, closer to 2 - 3 months depending on your usage.
The Huge Plus uses steel rollers over its predecessor’s ruby ball bearings. These are incredibly smooth compared to all over trackballs I’ve used (tbh haven’t tried many), but it can feel slightly scratchy/louder with very quick movements.

Can you use it to game? Absolutely, but if you haven’t used trackballs before, it’ll take a lot of practice to get used to it. I can play just about any game with it and still do fairly well, but full disclosure, I still switch to a traditional mouse if I’m going to take the game seriously due to the muscle memory and familiarity of the mice I’ve used all my life.

The ergonomics are great and it’s very comfortable after a long day of usage being easier on the wrist and helps to me hold less tension in my should from no longer having to move a mouse across the desk.

There’s a removable lithium battery which can be easily replaced without tools. The rollers can be replaced with ball bearings and it even comes with a tool in a compartment within the mouse that lets you extract the roller modules so you can put in new ones. This is great for keeping your device working well over many years as bearings/rollers could potentially break down and batteries do deteriorate over time. Unlike other companies that restrict what you can do with your own device, Elecom lets you easily make the replacements rather than forcing you to buy a new device.

Now for the fun part, maintenance. Yeah, this was something very new to me that I did not think of when I first bought this mouse as I was used to traditional mice which did not have moving parts. This did not please my short form content addled brain and very nearly drove me to return it as I felt insulted having to perform this new 30-second task. The bearings/rollers will accumulate dirt and gunk over time which need to be cleaned every couple of months. It’s usually sufficient to wipe it down the bearing housing with a clean cloth. Afterwards, you then need to lubricate the bearings. You can go about this two ways.
The easy way is to just rub the trackball against your forehead/nose as the oils on your skin lubricate the bearings surprisingly well.
The other option is to buy some dry PTFE lubricant and apply a very ***tiny*** amount to the bearings and then allow it to dry fully before use. 
I’ve heard others talk about graphite powder lubricant, but since graphite is **conductive**, it could potentially cause issues if it gets into the internal electronics which I would avoid.


If you want to get one for yourself, you can find it on online retailers or direct through [Elecom](https://elecomusa.com/products/huge-plus).

6-Month Update

I've been using the Huge Plus for 6 months now and I've enjoyed using it so much that I even bought a second for my other workstation.
After getting used to it, I'm able to play just about any game I normally play, including first-person shooters, without much of a difference from using a traditional style mouse.
I do not regret the purchase in the least.
