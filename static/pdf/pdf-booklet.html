Frequently Asked Questions

How does booklet page order work when I print?

In the default 1x2 (Booklet) mode, two pages sit side by side on each side of a sheet in saddle-stitch order: the first sheet's front carries the last page and page 1, its back carries page 2 and the second-to-last page, and so on inward. Print double-sided with the flip-on-short-edge setting, fold the stack in half, and the pages read in sequence.

What if my page count isn't a multiple of 4?

The count is rounded up to the next multiple of 4 and the missing slots are left blank, which lands them at the end of the booklet. A 10-page document becomes a 12-page booklet on three sheets, and the preview marks the empty slots as blank.

Which paper sizes can I use?

Pick from Letter, Legal, Tabloid, Ledger, Executive, Folio, and the ISO A, B, and C series from A0 down to C7. Letter is the default, and each source page is scaled to fit its slot on the sheet with a small margin.

Should the output be portrait or landscape?

Auto picks landscape for booklet mode, so two portrait pages sit side by side on a landscape sheet, and portrait for the grid modes. You can force either orientation; the source pages are scaled proportionally to fit whichever you choose.

What are the 2x2, 2x4, and 4x4 grid modes?

They place 4, 8, or 16 pages per sheet in reading order rather than booklet order, which is useful for handouts or thumbnail proof sheets. Only 1x2 reorders pages for folding; for more grid layouts see N-Up PDF.

Can I rotate landscape pages so they fit the booklet?

The Source Rotation option turns every page 90 degrees clockwise or counter-clockwise before imposition, or alternates the direction between odd and even pages for documents scanned head-to-head. Click Generate Preview to check the layout before downloading.

Does the booklet lose quality?

No. Pages are embedded as vector content from the original PDF rather than rasterized, so text stays sharp at any zoom and the file prints at full quality.

Is my PDF uploaded to create the booklet?

No. The page reordering, scaling, and preview rendering all run in your browser, and the finished booklet PDF is saved from your device without passing through a server.
