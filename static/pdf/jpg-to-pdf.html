Frequently Asked Questions

Can I combine multiple JPG files into one PDF?

Yes. Select or drag in as many JPGs as you need and each image becomes its own full-size page in a single file named from_jpgs.pdf. Pages follow the order of the file list, so remove and re-add a file if you want it in a different spot.

What does the JPG quality setting actually change?

High Quality passes your original JPEG data through unchanged. Medium (the default) re-encodes each image at 75% JPEG quality and scales the longest side down to 2500 pixels if it's larger; Low uses 50% quality and a 1500 pixel limit. For phone photos, Medium is usually 40 to 60% smaller than High with no visible difference on screen.

Does it support JPEG 2000 (.jp2 and .jpx) files?

Yes. JPEG 2000 files are accepted alongside .jpg and .jpeg, which is useful for medical imaging and geospatial data. If your browser can't decode a JPEG 2000 file for recompression, the original file is embedded as is, whichever quality you chose.

What page size will my JPGs get in the PDF?

Each image is placed as a full-size page with the image's own proportions, so portrait photos give portrait pages and landscape scans give landscape pages. There's no fixed A4 or Letter option in this tool.

Which quality setting is best for scanned documents?

Medium works for most scans that will be read on screen or printed at normal size, since 2500 pixels on the long side keeps text crisp. Choose High if the scans must stay at full resolution for OCR or archiving, and Low for quick sharing where size matters more than detail.

Why were some of my files skipped?

Only .jpg, .jpeg, .jp2, and .jpx files are accepted, so PNG, HEIC, WebP, and other formats are left out with a notice. Use the matching converter for those, or Image to PDF for a mixed set of formats.

Can I make the PDF on my phone?

Yes. The tool runs in a mobile browser; tap the upload area to pick photos from your camera roll. Medium quality keeps the resulting PDF a reasonable size for a large set of photos.

Are my photos uploaded to create the PDF?

No. Recompression happens on a canvas in your browser and the PDF is assembled by the PyMuPDF engine running as WebAssembly, so the photos never leave your device.
